# Tipping Your Barber in Ontario: What's Normal in 2026

Nobody explains barbershop etiquette, which is why so many people quietly worry about getting it wrong. The tipping question in particular has become more confusing as card terminals default to suggesting 18%, 22%, and 25% on services that used to run on cash and rough arithmetic.

Here's the straightforward version, plus the rest of the unwritten rules.

## The Standard: 15% to 20%

In Ontario, the customary tip for a barber is **15% to 20% of the service price**. That's the same range as restaurants, and it's what most barbers expect.

On common Mississauga prices, that works out to:

| Service | Price | 15% | 20% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Men's haircut | $30 | $4.50 | $6 |
| Beard trim | $25 | $3.75 | $5 |
| Haircut & beard | $50 | $7.50 | $10 |
| Hot towel shave | $25 | $3.75 | $5 |
| Kids' cut | $25 | $3.75 | $5 |
| Full package | $110 | $16.50 | $22 |

Most people round to a convenient number. On a $30 cut, $5 is the overwhelmingly common tip and lands comfortably in range.

**Above 20%** is for something exceptional — the barber fixed a bad cut from somewhere else, stayed late, squeezed you in, or did something with a difficult request that genuinely impressed you.

**Below 15%** signals dissatisfaction, whether or not you intend it to. If something went wrong, the more useful response is to say so directly. Barbers would rather fix a cut than be tipped less for it.

## Do You Tip the Owner?

The old convention was that you don't tip a shop owner, because they're taking the full price rather than a commission split.

That convention is largely gone. In practice, most people tip owners the same as anyone else, and most owner-barbers accept it without comment. Nobody will think worse of you either way, and if you're a regular, tipping the owner consistently is normal.

## Tipping on Walk-Ins, Kids' Cuts, and Discounts

**Walk-ins** get the same tip as appointments. The service is identical.

**Kids' cuts** get tipped at the same percentage, and it's worth being generous here — a squirming four-year-old is harder work than an adult, not easier.

**Discounted or promotional prices** should be tipped on the **regular price**, not the discounted one. If a $30 cut was $20 for a promotion, tip on $30. The barber did the full job.

**Senior rates** follow the same logic.

**Multiple people at once** — if you're paying for the whole family, tip on the total.

## Cash or Card?

Both work. Card is fine and increasingly standard, and there's no real stigma to using the terminal.

That said, cash tips reach the barber immediately and in full, without processing delays or, at some shops, a split. If you have cash on you, handing it directly is the small courtesy that barbers notice. If you don't, the card is genuinely fine.

## About Those Terminal Prompts

Card machines suggesting 25% as the middle option is a real phenomenon and it makes people feel cornered. You are not obligated to accept the default. Hitting "custom" and entering 15% or 18% is completely normal and nobody watching the screen thinks anything of it.

The percentages on the terminal are set by software defaults, not by the barber's expectations.

## The Rest of Barbershop Etiquette

Tipping is the question people ask. These are the ones they don't.

**Arrive on time, not early.** Ten minutes early is considerate. Thirty minutes early means you're sitting in a small space while someone else has their appointment.

**Arrive with clean hair.** You don't need to wash it that morning, but a barber cutting through three days of product, dust, or dry shampoo is working against you. Most shops offer a wash if you need one — at Clip & Chill it's $20 with styling.

**Cancel properly.** If you can't make it, call. A no-show costs a barber the full hour with no way to fill it. Most shops ask for a few hours' notice, and most will be entirely fine about a genuine last-minute problem if you tell them.

**Sit still and keep your head where they put it.** Barbers move your head deliberately. Following that, and resisting the urge to tilt down to see the mirror, makes a measurable difference to the result.

**Put the phone down for the fade.** Looking at a screen tilts your head forward, which changes the angle of your neckline. Barbers will ask; save them having to.

**Speak up during, not after.** If something isn't going the way you expected, say so while you're still in the chair. Adjustments are easy then and impossible later.

**Talk or don't.** Some people want conversation, some want twenty quiet minutes. Barbers read this quickly, and neither is rude. "I'm going to zone out for a bit if that's alright" is a perfectly acceptable sentence.

**Don't bring a beverage you can't put down**, and don't chew gum — both move the head.

**If you're bringing a child, bring an activity.** A tablet or a toy makes the appointment shorter and easier for everyone, including the child.

**Go back to the same barber.** The second cut with a barber is almost always better than the first, because they now know how your hair grows, where the cowlicks are, and what you meant last time.

## Leave a Review Instead of Over-Tipping

If a barber did outstanding work, a Google review is worth more to them than an extra five dollars. It's free, it takes ninety seconds, and for an independent shop it's the single most valuable thing a happy client can do.

Mentioning your barber by name in the review is even better — it's how individual barbers build a client base.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much do you tip a barber in Ontario?**
15% to 20% is standard. On a $30 haircut, that's $4.50 to $6, and most people round to $5.

**Do you tip a barber who owns the shop?**
Most people do, and it's the current norm. The older convention that owners aren't tipped has largely faded.

**Should I tip on a $30 haircut?**
Yes — $5 is the common tip and sits within the standard range.

**Is it rude not to tip a barber?**
Tipping is customary rather than required, but it's a well-established norm in Ontario, and a consistent non-tip is generally read as dissatisfaction. If you can't tip, that's understood; nobody will refuse to cut your hair.

**What if I didn't like my haircut?**
Say so before you leave. Most shops will adjust it on the spot at no charge. A silent low tip communicates displeasure without giving anyone a chance to fix it.

**Do I tip for a beard trim as well as the haircut?**
Tip on the total bill, not per service.
