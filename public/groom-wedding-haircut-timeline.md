# Wedding Haircut Timeline for Grooms: When to Book Every Appointment

There are photographs of this day that will exist for the rest of your life, and the single most common grooming mistake grooms make is getting a haircut too close to the wedding. The second most common is trying something new the week of.

Both are entirely avoidable with a schedule. Here's the one barbers actually recommend.

## Three Months Out: The Trial Run

If you're considering any change — growing a beard, going shorter, trying a different style — start now.

Three months gives you time to try a cut, live with it for a few weeks, see it in photographs, and change course if it's wrong. It's also long enough to grow a genuine beard from clean-shaven if that's the plan, since a full beard takes two to four months and passes through an awkward stage on the way.

This is also when to book your barber for the wedding week itself, especially if you're getting married in peak season. Good barbers fill up, and a Saturday morning slot in June is not something to leave to the week before.

## Six Weeks Out: The Shape

Get the cut you intend to have on the day. Not a variation of it — the actual cut.

This appointment tells you two things. First, whether the style suits you in practice rather than in theory. Second, exactly how it looks at various points in its grow-out cycle, which is the information you need to time the final cut.

Take photos, including from the side and back. Photos reveal things mirrors don't, and wedding photography is mostly angles you never see of yourself.

If you're growing a beard, this is the first proper shaping appointment. It establishes the outline that the next six weeks will build on.

## Two to Three Weeks Out: The Second-Last Cut

A maintenance cut that keeps the shape without resetting your timeline.

If you're doing a skin fade, this matters more — fades grow out visibly in two to three weeks, so this appointment sets up the final one correctly rather than leaving the barber to fix a badly grown-out fade three days before the wedding.

## Five to Seven Days Out: The Wedding Haircut

This is the important one, and the number is not arbitrary.

Hair cut the day before or two days before looks *cut*. The edges are stark, the line-up is razor-sharp in a way that reads as fresh rather than natural, and any small unevenness hasn't had time to settle. Five to seven days of growth softens all of that. The cut still looks crisp — it just looks like your hair rather than like a haircut.

There's also a practical argument: if something goes wrong, a week gives you time to fix it. Three days does not.

**The specific instruction to give your barber:** "This is for my wedding on [date] — cut it so it looks right in a week, not today." Every barber understands this request and will leave slightly more length accordingly.

Bring your photos from the six-week appointment so there's no ambiguity about what you're reproducing.

**Do not try anything new at this appointment.** Not a different fade height, not a shorter top, not a first-time beard shape. Whatever you liked at six weeks, repeat exactly.

## Two to Three Days Out: Beard Shape

If you have a beard, this is when to shape it — closer than the haircut, because beard hair grows faster and a beard shaped a week out will have lost its edges by the wedding.

Two to three days lets the lines settle without losing definition. Same rule applies: no new shapes.

If you're clean-shaven for the wedding, skip this appointment.

## The Day Before or the Morning Of: The Shave

A hot towel shave is the last item on the list, and the only one that should happen close to the day.

Timing depends on your growth rate. If you have heavy stubble by 5pm daily, book the morning of. If your growth is slower, the day before is fine and takes some pressure off the wedding morning.

A professional straight-razor shave gets closer than a home shave and, done properly, leaves the skin calmer — which matters more than usual when there's a camera involved. Book it as an appointment rather than a walk-in; wedding mornings have no slack in them.

**One caution:** if you've never had a hot towel shave before, don't have your first one on the wedding morning. Try one at the six-week or two-week appointment. Skin reacts differently to a straight razor, and you want to know how yours responds before the day.

## What Not to Do

**Don't get a haircut the day before.** The most common regret in wedding grooming.

**Don't try a new style within a month.** If it's wrong, you're out of runway.

**Don't experiment with new skincare in the final two weeks.** Reactions and breakouts take longer than you have to resolve. Whatever your routine is, keep it steady.

**Don't get a spray tan or facial treatment the day before a shave.** Both interact badly with a razor.

**Don't skip the eyebrows, ears, and nose.** In photographs, these are far more visible than most men realize. A face wax or threading takes ten minutes and covers all of it. At most shops this is inexpensive and quick — worth adding to the wedding-week appointment.

## The Complete Schedule

| Timing | Appointment |
|---|---|
| 3 months out | Trial cut; start beard if growing one; book wedding-week slot |
| 6 weeks out | The exact cut you'll have. Take photos. Beard shaped. |
| 2–3 weeks out | Maintenance cut |
| 5–7 days out | The wedding haircut |
| 2–3 days out | Beard shape and lineup |
| Day before / morning of | Hot towel shave, face wax or threading |

## For the Wedding Party

If groomsmen are getting cut too, book them together and book early. A group of five walking in on a Saturday morning without appointments is how a wedding-day schedule falls apart.

Most shops will coordinate multiple chairs for a group if you give them notice. Call rather than booking five separate online slots — it's easier for everyone and the shop can sequence it properly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long before a wedding should I get my hair cut?**
Five to seven days. Long enough for the cut to soften and settle, short enough that it still holds its shape.

**Should I get a beard trim the same day as the haircut?**
You can, but shaping the beard two to three days before the wedding gives a sharper result, because beard hair grows faster than the timeline for a haircut allows.

**Is a hot towel shave worth it for a wedding?**
It's the closest, cleanest shave available and it leaves skin in better condition than a rushed home shave. Just don't make the wedding morning your first one.

**What if I want to grow a beard for the wedding?**
Start three to four months out. That gives you time to get through the awkward stage, establish a shape, and decide whether you actually like it with enough runway to shave it off if you don't.
