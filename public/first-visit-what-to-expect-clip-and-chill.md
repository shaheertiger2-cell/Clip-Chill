# Your First Visit to Clip & Chill: What to Expect

Trying a new barbershop involves a small amount of unnecessary uncertainty — how it works, whether you need to book, what it costs, and whether you'll be able to explain what you want to someone who's never cut your hair.

Here's the whole thing, start to finish, so none of that is a question.

## Before You Arrive

**You don't need an appointment.** Walk-ins are welcome every day we're open. That said, if you're coming on a Saturday, a Sunday afternoon, or a weekday after 5pm, booking is worth the minute it takes — those are our busiest windows.

**Bring a reference photo if you have one.** Describing a haircut in words is harder than it sounds, and a photo removes most of the ambiguity. Ideally one where you can see the fade height and the length on top, on hair reasonably similar to yours.

**Come with reasonably clean hair.** Not necessarily washed that morning, but a barber cutting through three days of product is working against you. If you'd rather we wash it, that's a $20 wash-and-style service.

## When You Walk In

Check in at the front, whether you booked or not — you're not in the queue until someone knows you're there. If there's a wait, we'll give you a realistic estimate rather than an optimistic one, and you're welcome to leave your number and come back rather than sitting.

**We don't double-book.** Every client gets their barber's full attention for the length of the service. It's the main reason the cuts come out consistent rather than rushed, and it's why the wait estimate we give you is usually accurate.

## The Consultation

This is the part that determines everything else, and it takes about two minutes.

Your barber will ask some version of:

- What are you looking for today?
- How long since your last cut?
- How do you style it day to day?
- What didn't you like about the last one?

Answer the last two honestly, including "I don't style it at all" and "it was too short at the back." Both are useful information. A barber who knows you have four minutes and no products in the morning will cut something that works with that rather than something that needs styling you'll never do.

If you don't know what you want, say exactly that. "Something clean and low-maintenance — what would suit my hair?" is a completely normal opener and often produces a better result than a vague description.

If you're not sure whether to go skin fade or taper, or how short to go, ask. You'll get a straight answer, including if the thing you're asking for isn't going to work on your hair or your hairline.

## The Cut

Roughly what to expect on timing:

- **Haircut** — about 30 minutes
- **Beard trim** — about 15 minutes
- **Haircut and beard** — about 45 minutes
- **Hot towel shave** — about 30 minutes
- **Kids' cut** — about 30 minutes
- **Clip & Chill Package** — about 1 hour 30 minutes

A few things that help during: keep your head where the barber puts it, put the phone down during the fade (looking down changes your neckline angle), and speak up if something isn't going the way you expected. Adjustments are easy while you're in the chair and impossible once you've left.

Talk or don't — both are completely fine. "I'm going to zone out for a bit" is a normal sentence in a barbershop.

At the end, your barber will show you the back with a mirror. Actually look. If you want it tighter, shorter, or the neckline squared differently, say so then.

## After Your First Cut

Two suggestions that make every subsequent visit better.

**Judge the cut at day five, not day one.** Almost any haircut looks fine walking out. What matters is whether it still holds its shape most of the way through your cycle — that's the thing a good cut does and a rushed one doesn't.

**Book your next one before you leave**, with the same barber. The second cut is better than the first because they now know how your hair grows, and the fifth is better than the second. That consistency is the entire reason to have a regular barber rather than a regular barbershop.
