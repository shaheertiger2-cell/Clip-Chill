# Prom and Grad Haircut Guide for Mississauga Students

Prom photos last considerably longer than prom does. The haircut in them is one of the few things you fully control, and the two mistakes that get made every single year are avoidable: getting cut too close to the day, and trying something brand new the week of.

Here's the timeline that works.

## The Timeline

**Six weeks out — the trial.** If you're considering anything different from your usual cut, do it now. Six weeks gives you time to wear it, see it in photos, and change direction if it's not right. It also tells you exactly how the style looks at two, four, and six weeks of growth, which is the information you need to time the real one.

**Two to three weeks out — a maintenance cut.** Keeps the shape without resetting anything. This matters more if you're getting a fade, because a fade grown out for six weeks isn't something a barber can fix perfectly three days before prom.

**Five to seven days out — the prom cut.** This is the important one. Hair cut the day before looks *cut* — the edges are stark and any small unevenness hasn't settled. Five to seven days of growth softens it into looking like your hair, which photographs far better. It also leaves time to fix anything you're not happy with.

The sentence to say when you book: **"This is for prom on [date] — cut it so it looks right in a week, not today."** Every barber understands the request and will adjust accordingly.

**Two to three days out — beard shape**, if you have one. Beard hair grows faster than the haircut timeline allows, so shaping it closer keeps the lines sharp.

**The morning of — nothing new.** Wash, style the way you've practised, done. Prom morning is not the time to discover a new product.

## What Photographs Well

Prom photography is mostly flash, mostly close, and mostly from the front. That changes what works.

**Matte, not shiny.** Flash photography turns shine into glare. A product that looks glossy in your bathroom mirror looks wet in a photo. Matte clay or matte paste.

**Structure over softness.** Cuts with definition — a taper or fade with clear shape on top — read better in photos than something soft and undefined, which can look flat under direct light.

**Don't go too short.** Extremely short cuts photograph fine, but if you're going shorter than usual, do it at the six-week trial, not five days before. Short cuts leave you no options.

**Check yourself under a flash before the day.** Take a photo with your phone flash on, in a mirror, once your hair is styled. It takes ten seconds and it's a completely different image from what the mirror shows you.

## Cuts That Work

**A mid taper fade with textured length on top.** The most-requested style for a reason — it's sharp, current, and it photographs with real definition.

**A classic side part.** Formal, deliberate, and it suits a suit. If you're going traditional with the outfit, this is the matching cut.

**A textured crop.** Modern, low-effort to style, and it holds up through a long night.

**Curly hair: a fade with the curls kept up top.** Don't flatten curls for a formal event — the contrast between tight sides and defined curls is one of the best-looking options available, and it survives four hours better than anything that depends on staying in place.

**What to skip:** anything requiring more than three minutes of styling, anything you've never worn before, and anything you need to keep touching. Touching your hair all night is how a style ends up worse at 10pm than it was at 6pm.

## Beards and Faces at 17 and 18

**If your growth is patchy** — which is completely normal at this age — don't try to force a full beard for prom. Either shave clean or keep deliberate stubble at a consistent length with a tidy neckline. Both look sharper than a full beard with gaps.

**If you have real growth**, get it shaped two to three days before. The neckline and cheek line are what make a beard look intentional rather than uncut, and they're worth having done properly.

**If you're shaving clean**, shave the morning of rather than the night before if your growth is heavy. And if you've never had a hot towel shave, don't make prom morning your first — try one at the six-week appointment so you know how your skin responds.

**Eyebrows, ears, nose.** More visible in photos than anyone expects. Threading or a face wax takes about ten minutes and handles all of it.

## Skin

Not the fun part, but it matters more than the haircut in a close-up.

- **Don't change your routine in the final two weeks.** New products cause reactions and breakouts, and you don't have time to resolve them.
- **Don't pick at anything.** A spot heals in days; a picked spot photographs for weeks.
- **Moisturize daily** for the two weeks before, so skin doesn't look dry under flash.
- **Sleep and water** in the final week actually help, boring as that is.

## Grad Photos Are a Separate Appointment

Worth noting because people forget: grad photos are often taken weeks or months before prom, sometimes in the fall. Those photos are in the yearbook permanently.

Same rule applies — cut five to seven days before, matte product, nothing new. If your grad photo date and prom are months apart, they're two separate haircuts to plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long before prom should I get a haircut?**
Five to seven days. Long enough for the cut to settle and stop looking brand new, short enough that it's still holding shape, with a buffer to fix anything.

**Should I try a new hairstyle for prom?**
Only if you trial it about six weeks ahead. Trying something new the week of prom is how people end up unhappy in photos that last forever.

**What haircut photographs best?**
Something with definition — a taper or fade with texture on top — styled with a matte product. Shine glares under flash.

**Should I have a beard for prom?**
If your growth is full, get it shaped two to three days before. If it's patchy, clean-shaven or tidy stubble looks sharper than a full beard with gaps.
